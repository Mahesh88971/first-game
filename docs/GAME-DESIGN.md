# Wildline Racing

## The first event

An island fishing cooperative opens its service roads for the Breakwater Circuit. Drivers start between working boathouses, follow the highway to the tidal flats and return over the forest ridge as a squall rolls in. A legible dry racing line, a quick wet line and two fork decisions give a player something to learn on each lap. Orange marshal boards, cream typography and deep green coastal imagery identify the series.

The first release target is a solo Windows race on Tropical Storm Coast. Three laps of 6.4 km at an average of 80–125 km/h take roughly 3–5 minutes per lap. Eight opponents start ahead of the player. The same finish line records every entrant. The player can finish in any position, collect a purse and retry immediately.

## Driving loop

1. Read the surface, bend and upcoming traffic from the chase camera.
2. Choose a line, lift or brake, then steer. The handbrake loosens the rear while steering assistance guides the vehicle along the course.
3. Earn energy from controlled slides, slipstreaming and clean landings. Spend it on a straight or on the exit from a bend.
4. Cross a roadside induction strip to carry one ability. Deploy it when grip, damage or track position gives it value.
5. Review the lap splits and rivals at the finish. Change chassis or tune the garage loadout, then test a different route.

## Chassis and terrain

| Chassis | Distinguishing feel | Favoured line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dunefox XR, off-road buggy | Low mass, long travel, quick rotation and progressive slide | Sand, tidal cut and rough landings |
| Strada RS, rally car | Strong acceleration, most road speed and sharp braking | Asphalt apexes and bridge |
| Atlas ST, sports SUV | High torque, slower weight transfer and impact resistance | Mud, broken shoulders and contact |

Wet asphalt loses lateral grip gradually. Sand increases rolling resistance. Mud combines drag and low grip. Shallow water adds depth-dependent drag, lateral wave force and spray. A hard shoulder strike costs momentum. Timber crates splinter at speed. Damage primarily affects appearance, with a small, capped performance cost. Repair strips and field repair restore the car.

## Course book

| Part of lap | Place | Driving decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8% | Fishing village | Thread the grid, sheds and loading crates |
| 8–22% | Coast highway | Draft on asphalt and set up for the tidal cut |
| 22–31% | Beach | Trade road speed for a sandy inside line |
| 31–37% | Tidal crossing | Keep the car straight through a wave |
| 37–47% | Palm forest | Watch the verge for timber and loose ground |
| 47–54% | Timber bridge | Stay between the rails |
| 54–63% | Rock tunnel | Follow the lamps to the portal |
| 63–72% | Storm ridge | Manage grip as the squall arrives |
| 72–83% | Mud trail | Use the ruts and save energy for the crest |
| 83–90% | Cliff jump | Align the wheels before landing |
| 90–96% | Waterfall | Take the wet bypass or the outside road |
| 96–100% | Return to harbour | Defend on the approach to the timing line |

Both short routes are continuous pieces of road with an entry and a rejoin. Timing compares equivalent progress on the main route. The shoreline cut trades distance for water resistance. The waterfall cut trades distance for a tighter, rougher channel. A signed launch ramp, flooded tidal section and lightning over the ridge provide repeatable landmarks.

## Competition and abilities

Eight named drivers vary in target pace, passing side, risk tolerance and shortcut use. A driver reads curvature, competitors and hazards ahead. All competitors integrate the same vehicle simulation. The field receives no position teleportation. The early lap provides room to learn without a reset following a mistake.

Induction strips supply one held ability. A leader tends to receive shield or traction. Trailing racers can receive nitro or pulse. A damaged chassis favours repair. Pulse affects nearby competitors within a bounded radius, shield absorbs a hit, and traction temporarily reduces slip. Abilities use the same durations for human and AI drivers.

## Garage and progression

All three slice vehicles are available for comparison from the start. Finish rewards combine a participation purse and placement bonus. Best lap, best race, starts completed, XP, credits, equipped chassis and acquired upgrades persist locally. Earned credits buy a one-time chassis tune. Finishing outside the podium still funds progress. The career design extends from regional events through off-road, street, extreme, international and world championships. Additional regions, ranked play and seasonal economies follow vehicle and course validation.

## Screens and input

Race hub → vehicle selection or course briefing → race grid → three laps → results → hub or rematch. Garage and settings are directly accessible from the hub. A pause sheet provides controls, resume, restart and return. The racing HUD prioritizes position, lap, timer and speed. Gear, RPM, boost, damage, held ability, sector and minimap sit around the periphery. Keyboard and touch controls share action values.

## Asset requirements

The playable needs three original vehicle silhouettes, four independent wheel poses per car, a helmeted driver for the buggy, start gantry, timber village kit, utility lines, palms, broadleaf plants, boats, tunnel lining, bridge rails, terrain ribbons, route markers, obstacles, atmospheric backdrop, original engine voices and surface effects. The hub and garage use original generated photographic key art. Live racing uses real-time models. Production art acceptance adds scanned surface detail, texture streaming, authored damage morphs, skeletal suspension rigs, measured audio recordings and a physically validated night-lighting pass.
